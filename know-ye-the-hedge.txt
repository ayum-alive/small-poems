Know ye the hedge where the bracken and bramble
Are emblems of deeds that are done in the night?
Where the snuffle of snout and the slow, sleepy shamble
Go off with a rustle and end in a fright?
Know ye the leaf-pile, the beetle, the slug,
Where the wind in the ivy is cosy and snug;
Where the spines of the tenant, rolled up in a ball,
Say "no" to the fox and "no" to us all --
'Tis the clime of the hedge, 'tis the land of the snail:
Oh! wild as the sneeze of a hog in a gale.

(after Byron's Bride of Abydos, which turned up in my finds)
